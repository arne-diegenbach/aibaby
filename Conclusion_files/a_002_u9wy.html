Repeats But Cannot Name

A simulated infant learns to imitate a word, and can be taught a vowel by praise alone. It cannot learn to say one thing for one object and another for another — and after eliminating delivery, perception, credit assignment, motor readout, teaching protocol and homeostasis, what is left is conditionality itself.

01 — THE SETUP

One creature, three goals, two of them met

A leaky integrate-and-fire network with reward-modulated STDP and node perturbation, wired to a cochlea, a retina and a formant synthesiser. It babbles, hears itself, sleeps, and is taught by a caregiver who says one of two words.

Three behavioural goals were set. G1, determinism: the same genome and inputs give a bit-identical brain. G2, reward shapes behaviour: praise moves what the creature does. G3, cross-modal association: the creature says one thing for a cube and another for a ball.

G1 and G2 hold. Three further milestones hold with them, each against a yoked control that receives the same praise in the same proportions for nothing it did:

M1b — the creature repeats a word met 0.890 held-out, 5/5 seeds, audible at d′ 1.37. Scored 200–600 ms after the word stops, where the ear reads at chance and the voice still carries it.
M1c — the creature is taught a sound met Praise alone moves its vowel toward a target it never hears: +15.9 points against its yoke, d′ 5.57 against a −0.01 null, 3/3 seeds.
M1d — the creature answers when spoken to met +0.349 answering burst, 6/6 seeds, and nearly silent while listening (0.276 → 0.010). The answer carries the word +0.215 above ambient babble, 3/3.
G3 — the creature names what it sees not met Closed negative under every mechanism tried across DNA v12–v49, including three built specifically for it in the final week.
02 — THE MEASUREMENT

The same reward, the same act, made conditional

The sharpest form of the result does not involve naming at all. It takes G2's own contingency — the one reward demonstrably shapes — and asks for it conditionally.

The creature is rewarded for pushing a scalar past a criterion set at its own baseline median, so the target is defined by the creature rather than by the experimenter. Unconditionally, reward moves that boundary hard. Conditionally — push it up after word A, down after word B — it does not move at all, against a control that shares the arm's marginals exactly.

Reward moves an unconditional act at twelve sigma and the same act made conditional does not move at all.

The control this needed, and did not have until late. An earlier version scored the conditional arm against a yoke built from the taught arm's reward stream — a mismatched comparison that manufactured a +16.4 result and a clean dose–response. Giving each arm its own yoke and a matched-marginal random target collapsed it to −0.1 ± 0.7. The dose–response had been the control moving. The published finding was retracted.
03 — THE ASYMMETRY

A heard word reaches the voice. A seen object does not.

The creature has one conditional route that works — ear to larynx, which is what makes it able to repeat. Reading the same pipeline under the two conditions shows exactly where they part.

Each figure is a held-out classifier reading the module's population: can it tell which of two stimuli was present? One seed family is shown; across the three, the voice reads 0.860–0.920 for a heard word and 0.460–0.600 for a seen object.

Both input controls are lit — the stimulus is unambiguously present at the sensor in each case. The word survives the whole chain and comes out of the mouth. The object is legible at the retina at 0.980, is still measurably present in the larynx population, and arrives at the voice at chance.

This is not a delivery failure in the usual sense. A separately pinned measurement puts the seen object at the larynx at 0.667 per neuron, against 0.693 at central and 0.960 at the retina. The object does reach the motor population, at close to the strength it had one stage earlier. It does not reach the air.

04 — THE LEDGER

Six explanations, each with its own control

Every plausible account of the failure was given a measurement that could have confirmed it. None did. The order below is roughly the order they were believed.

The object never reaches the larynx refuted Built the tract the genome lacked (vision→vocal, shipped) and an arcuate vision→auditory transcoder. Object at auditory 0.880; voice moved +0.04 / 0.00 / −0.06. The best-delivering creature ever built moves the milestone 0.487 → 0.546.
The ear has no real word categories refuted One-of-eight at the auditory module reads 0.981 (0.981 / 0.962 / 1.000) against a 1.000 mel ceiling, shuffled control 0.122 vs chance 0.125. No headroom. The 0.371 that motivated a new coder is the creature's voice, not its ear.
Credit is assigned to the wrong synapses refuted A per-neuron reward-mask oracle removes lesson interference completely (targeted retention ≈1.0, 3/3) at ~30% of the learning rate — and naming still does not work with it (−0.045 against no mask). Node perturbation was already assigning credit correctly; the problem was variance.
The motor readout is too lossy refuted Replaced the centroid readout with a learnable dictionary of postures plus a categorical policy gradient. Five dictionary configurations, none teachable; two centroid configurations, both. The pooling stage is what makes the larynx steerable.
The condition arrives on too noisy a baseline refuted Built a zero-baseline context module. Reward's write share on that tract goes 1–7% → 28–79%, the most learnable input in the creature — and the positive control dies at every driven level, on 5 genomes and 12 levels. Arriving costs more than it pays.
The teaching protocol is wrong refuted 28× longer sessions: −0.035 ± 0.013. Blocked rather than interleaved teaching: −0.146, 3/3 — actively harmful, because naming's two lessons share one output dimension.
Rate homeostasis erases the conditional map refuted Intrinsic plasticity does respond, but it saturates rather than regulates: 25–83% of the larynx ends a driven session pinned at threshold_max, threshold climbing +1.2 to +2.8 into a [0.20, 4.00] clamp. The graded re-regulation story does not hold.

Two further families were tried as learning rules rather than as explanations, and both are informative in the same direction. Perturbation moved onto synapses instead of neurons cut learning noise from 65% to 8% and still could not carry even G2 — so expressiveness was never the constraint. And a burst/plateau third factor discriminated the object at 0.673, the best any third factor has managed here, while the plateau it derives from reads 0.913: the nonlinearity costs more than it delivers.

05 — WHAT IS LEFT

The wall is conditionality, not any of its parts

The condition arrives. The credit is assigned correctly. The reward moves the act. The motor plant is demonstrably steerable, and the readout that makes it steerable is the one that was suspected of losing the signal. What does not happen, under any of it, is the dependence — the output conditioning on which stimulus was present.

The creature does have one conditional route, and it works well: the innate ear-to-larynx path that produces imitation at 0.890. Every attempt to build a second one that reward can shape has failed at the same place, and the eligibility trace shows why the failure is not a failure of signal. On the tract that actually participates, the trace reads the object at 0.797 conditionality against a 0.867 positive control. The information is in the trace. Two traces recorded under the two conditions are nonetheless 94.5% identical.

A differential this small on a common mode this large is not a signal-to-noise problem you can solve by delivering more signal. It is the arithmetic of the rule.

Stated carefully: in this architecture, a presynaptic-gated three-factor rule cannot build a conditional map on a population that is simultaneously hosting reward-driven exploration. That is narrower than "spiking networks cannot learn associations", and it is what the measurements support.

06 — METHOD

The part that transfers

Most of the work in a negative result is making the negative trustworthy. Seven practices did more for this project than any mechanism in it.

Measure the floor before believing the finding practice The naming milestone's control genome swings ±0.060 across seed families, giving a 0.120 cross-family floor. That column was quoted as a result for months before anyone measured what it does when nothing is being taught.
A control must share the arm's marginals practice Yoked controls receive identical praise and scolding in identical proportions for nothing they did. Without that, "reward arrived" and "reward taught" are indistinguishable — and a mismatched yoke produced a +16.4 result that did not survive.
A positive control gates its own row practice A level where the known-good arm has collapsed cannot report a null on anything else: it is a different creature being asked the same question. Three experiments printed wrong verdicts before this became a gate rather than a habit.
Derive the constant, do not sample it practice Intrinsic plasticity steps a threshold by ip_rate × (rate − target), so the drift is the integral of the error. Derived: +3.12 ± 0.21 Hz. Sampled off the final EMA: 8.18 ± 2.48 Hz for the same quantity.
Never move a threshold to fit the data practice A binary verdict that flips on one seed of three means the quantity is continuous and the label was the mistake. Print the ratio; leave the threshold alone until the seeds agree.
A cheap run is a different measurement, not a weaker one practice The homeostasis probe reads 0% pinned at 200k ticks and grants its licence; at 3.4M it reads 25–83% and refuses. Three seeds with unanimous signs (+0.24) became +0.03 at six. A single-seed pass once hid a 4-of-9 failure.
Pin the numbers decisions rest on practice A command re-derives every load-bearing figure and fails on drift, the way a hash check fails on a changed build. It exists because two stale documented numbers caused a mechanism to be built against a bottleneck that had already been closed.
The failure mode all seven guard against is the same one. Every wrong verdict in this project was plausible when printed. None looked like a bug; each looked like a finding. The instruments that earned their cost are the ones that can refuse — a probe whose only interesting outcome is "do not build this" saved a rewrite stage in thirty seconds, by measuring the thing everyone had assumed.
07 — FALSIFIERS

What would change the conclusion

The result is stated so it can be overturned. Any of these would do it:

  • Separate populations. In birdsong, the timing pathway and the exploratory pathway are distinct afferents onto a shared motor nucleus. Here one population is asked to be both. The context-module result — the condition arriving costs more than it pays — is what that architecture would predict, and the split was never built.
  • A rule whose gate is not presynaptic activity. Every mechanism here factorises credit into a term shared across conditions plus a small differential. Seven separate walls have that same arithmetic.
  • An output space where the two lessons do not share a dimension. The creature holds two lessons at once on orthogonal targets (0.84 retained versus 0.22 for conflicting ones). Naming's two lessons compete for one dimension, which is also why blocked teaching hurts.
  • Any conditional result that survives a matched-marginal control. Several did not.
