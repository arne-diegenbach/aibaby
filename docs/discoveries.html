The aibaby project — a C++17 spiking neural creature grown from a DNA/genome file

10 October 2026 (revised)

Abstract

This document collects the findings of the aibaby project that are not held up by any published result. The project grows a spiking neural “creature” from a genome file and teaches it to produce vowel formants; much of its machinery is named after the literature it came from, and those mechanisms are deliberately excluded here. What remains is the set of things the system itself was the first to say: measured quantities with no external reference, structural obstacles that only appear in this architecture, and a group of inference failures that were found by being committed and then caught. The longest single thread is §4: because the readout is a rate-weighted centroid, the influence of one neuron on behaviour can be differentiated rather than estimated, and that derivative — taken with a sign, which is the part the data forced — accounts for a measured asymmetry, its reversal between targets, and why one restriction on plasticity costs half the lesson while another binds a third of all synapses and costs nothing.

The methodological findings are listed first and at length, because they are the most transferable part and the least specific to a creature made of spikes. Several of them are reports of a mistake that survived review — a test no outcome could pass, a refusal criterion computed from the wrong arm, a statistic diagnosed in a comment and then quoted for months. They are included on the view that a failure mode with a worked example is more useful than a rule without one.

Every quantity below comes from a run gated on a pinned determinism hash, ad96f882becbee92, under which every mechanism that ships off is bit-identical. Section 8 gives provenance.

1. Scope, and what is excluded

A finding is included if the project discovered it, and excluded if a published result says it first or frames it. The line is drawn at the claim, not at the tools: the estimators used throughout are Cochran’s ratio estimator and the Quenouille–Tukey jackknife, and the quantities they produce are still this project’s, so they appear here while the methods do not.

Excluded on that basis are: the account of interference as failed contextual inference (Heald, Lengyel and Wolpert’s COIN model), the finding that context gating alone is insufficient without synaptic stabilisation (Masse, Grant and Freedman), importance-weighted protection of earlier weights (Kirkpatrick et al.; Zenke, Poole and Ganguli), node perturbation as a motor learning rule (Fiete, Fee and Seung), the stability–plasticity dilemma (Grossberg), the uniform cluster prior implicit in MacQueen’s 1/wins rule, the division of a population code into output-potent and output-null dimensions (Kaufman, Churchland, Ryu and Shenoy), and activity-dependent myelination as a consolidation mechanism (the oligodendroglia literature, cited by title and DOI in the main list where author lists could not be verified). Each is cited in the project’s main reference list. Appendix A lists the project results that were excluded by this criterion, so the exclusion can be audited rather than taken on trust.

2. Measurement and inference

These came out of running the same experiment many times and being wrong in a way that printed a number. None has an external source; each has a worked example in the project history.

2.1 Chance has no fixed value when the readout is “nearest”

A k-alternative naming score read against 1/k is not a chance comparison. When the decision rule is nearest-target in formant space, chance depends on the geometry of the targets: the same uninformative voice scores 0.161 against targets on the vowel corners and 0.306 against a 2×2 arrangement. An informationless voice reads about 0.30 on a four-word task where 1/k would predict 0.25. One earlier ceiling claim was a null error of exactly this kind: a real effect scored against an arithmetic chance level while the matched control sat in the same table, unread.

2.2 A test that nearly all outcomes pass is not a test, and neither is one that none can pass

Two symmetric failures, both found after they had produced a published-looking verdict. A guard written as cd2 > −3·13.1 — “greater than −39.3” — passes on nearly any data, and was reported as “F2 is spared” when it meant “F2 is unmeasured”. In the other direction, an intervention’s success branch required a shift of 0.090 where a complete closure of the gap could produce only 0.070; it printed a confident conclusion from an unreachable branch. The fix in both cases is to print the power of the test beside its result, so that a criterion nothing could have cleared is visible rather than silent.

A third shape, and the one that hid longest: a criterion only the best possible result fails. An intervention’s success branch asked whether the residual effect was indistinguishable from zero, written as |residual| < 2 × its standard error. The intervention then worked perfectly — the residual came back exactly zero, so its standard error was exactly zero too, and 0 < 0 is false. Execution fell through to the weaker branch and printed “contributes but does not account for it” over numbers showing a complete closure at 5.5 SE. The two earlier instances were a criterion nothing could clear and one almost anything could, which is why neither suggested looking for this: here the test is passed by every outcome except the strongest. The general fix is to state a closure as a fraction of the quantity being closed rather than as a distance from zero, because the distance-from-zero form divides by a precision that a perfect result drives to nothing.

2.3 The reference point is the hypothesis’s own prediction, not zero

A hypothesis that names a value must be tested against that value. A drift account predicting a decay of −0.034 was first tested for being distinguishable from zero, which it was not, and so survived; tested against its own prediction, the measurement sat 3.9 SE away and the account was excluded. The same error in another guise is scoring an effect against 1/k while the real control is in the table.

2.4 A null and a reversal are different results

An else branch that catches both reports the wrong one. An intervention expected to close a gap was tested only for closure; when the gap widened, the outcome fell through to the null branch and was written up as “no effect”. The widening was 2.5 SE in the opposite direction to the prediction, which is a stronger result than the null it was reported as.

2.5 A constant copied instead of derived fails silently, and travels

Six instances in one 34,000-line file, in two families. Indices: a literal array size that was a copy of a named bound and overran into the next field; positional grid indices; positional arm indices that would have mislabelled whole rows; and pair indices (k·2, k·2+1) that silently shifted when two table rows were inserted above them. Labels: a band value copied beside the table it came from, so an experiment reported its widest condition as a tenth of its real width after the table was rescaled; and a deleted control whose label and refusal criterion were inherited by unrelated rows; and a single hardcoded baseline arm, correct while every arm shared one lesson target and silently wrong the moment a second target was added, which charged the new arms the whole difference between the two conditions — one cost read 11% against the old control and 3% against its own, and three of eight printed numbers were wrong.

What makes this class survive review is specific and worth stating: every site carried a comment acknowledging the indexing was positional, and the comment was treated as the fix. One even warned about a skipped row reading zero — a different hazard from a shifted row, against which the guard written for the first does nothing. A mislabelled number still prints with a standard error and still reads as a result, so nothing in the output looks wrong. The question that finds these is: is this label attached to the data, or merely next to it?

2.6 A passing determinism hash does not say the code is correct

The project gates every run on a pinned hash. A pass says the shipped configuration is unchanged and the run is reproducible; it says nothing about whether the experiment computes what its name claims. Four of the six errors in §2.5 were introduced and committed under a passing hash.

2.7 Reproduce the conditions, not the experiment name

A headline was called irreproducible after three runs at the registered default tick count. The recorded result had been taken at 3.5× that budget, which was written in its own log’s filename and in the note that cited it. A registered default is not evidence about what a recorded run used, and this system has a documented power curve across exactly that range — one experiment reads +1.0 at 560k ticks against +18.3 at 3.4M.

The same error in its archive form, and it is the easier one to commit. A ladder of mask extents was assembled by collecting arms from every log in the results directory with a single grep -h matching on the arm's name. One of the seven rows came from a pilot run whose own baseline arm gained +0.0363 where every other row’s baseline gained +0.3618 — a tenfold difference in the quantity being compared — and nothing in the pooled output recorded which log each line came from. A search across runs is a join on the arm name, and the arm name does not carry the budget. The rule that catches it is to read the anchor of the log a number comes from before reading the number, which this project applies to its own runs as a matter of course and had not applied to its own archive.

2.8 Diagnosing a broken statistic does not retire it

A retention measure was computed as a mean of per-creature ratios whose denominator — how much that creature learned — was floored only at 1e-6. The code carried a comment beside it recording that the statistic had read −5.587 ± 6.729 and 27.222 ± 21.160 on another genome and calling it “a broken statistic reporting nothing”, and a denominator-free replacement was added alongside. The headline, the milestone gate and the pre-registered specificity criterion all went on reading the broken one.

Measured on the same 128 sessions, the two estimators disagree in sign: a mean of ratios gives +0.182 ± 0.663 and a jackknifed ratio of sums gives −0.211 ± 0.074, with standard errors differing ninefold. The transferable part is not the statistic but the order of operations: a comment records a fact, it does not change what the gates compute, and every consumer has to be moved by hand. Grep for the variable, not for the comment.

2.9 Unanimous signs on three seeds are not power

Repeatedly, effects that were unanimous and large on a handful of creatures did not survive more of them. A smoothing sweep read +0.24 at n = 3 and +0.03 at n = 6; a salience-reward effect was +2.6 SE at n = 12 and null at n = 16; a vigilance score of 0.737 fell to 0.594 at 96 creatures. The project adopted a 3 SE bar at n ≤ 18 as a direct consequence, and the bar must be fixed once and named, not chosen per test: one verdict printed “not yet a finding” for 2.9 SE against a 3 SE bar and then made a 2.7 SE contrast its headline because that test had been coded against 2.0.

2.10 Two points agreeing to a tenth of a percent is one coincidence

Two lesson targets gave lever costs of 30.01% and 22.12% of the lesson, and the distance each had to move the readout stood in a ratio of 1.356 against the costs’ 1.357 — a match to 0.1%, from no fitted parameter. It was recorded as a coincidence on two points rather than a law, and the third target refused it: the law predicted 14.8% and the measurement was 25.0% ± 5.9%, and the three points are non-monotone in distance travelled (0.200 → 25.0%, 0.300 → 22.1%, 0.407 → 30.0%), which no proportionality can produce at all.

The transferable part is the arithmetic rather than the caution. Any smooth one-parameter family can be made to pass through two points, so a two-point agreement carries no information about the family; and the cheapest way to settle it was to measure a third point, which cost one overnight run, rather than to argue about the first two. The same project had already paid for the opposite choice once: a leverage law fitted to three costs with one free parameter matched them to 1.68 percentage points and then failed two further costs out of sample. Restricted to the points where the lever is the same neuron, the surviving pattern needs a large distance-independent term plus a weak distance term — two parameters on two points — so the account was stopped there instead.

2.11 A pre-registered prediction can be wrong in sign on both of its axes

Pre-registration fixes what a result will be compared against; it does not make the prediction right, and the failure mode worth recording is the one where a prediction fails in the direction nobody wrote a branch for. A cross-mechanism regularity — every plasticity restriction tried here cut learning more than it cut forgetting — was used to predict that removing a restriction would buy more learning than it cost in retention. The arm that removed it gained less and retained less. Both differences are nulls, so the regularity is not refuted at significance, but neither was it confirmed, and the registered text had no reading for “worse on both axes”. Registering the two directions a prediction could fail in is not enough when the mechanism can also simply fail to matter.

A second shape of the same failure, from a registration that offered two accounts and asked which one the data would pick. One said a correctly aimed quarter-sized mask would keep most of the learning of a half-sized one; the other said both quarters would cost more than the half and that aiming would buy nothing. The measurement gave both effects, separately resolved and of comparable size: aim was worth +0.0956 ± 0.0063 at matched size, and shrinking the mask at matched aim cost −0.0719 ± 0.0132. A dichotomy cannot be confirmed by a conjunction — it can only be half-refused on each side, which is what the write-up had to say. The discipline that would have survived is to register the quantities and the direction each should move, not a menu of accounts to choose between.

2.12 Grep the results directory before building the experiment

Three times in one week the answer to a question this project was about to spend hours measuring was already in its own output. A symmetric account of leverage was refuted by a run on disk, not by a new one. A law relating cost to distance was refused by measuring a third point rather than by arguing about the first two. And a plan to re-measure a cost curve at the opposite end of the motor group — written up, committed, costed at about five hours — turned out to be an experiment that had run three weeks earlier, whose summary was filed in the note immediately adjacent to the one being corrected, and which the same day’s work had cited for a different number.

The last of the three is the instructive one, because the obstacle was not that the result was hard to find. It was that the question had been framed as a lead, and a lead reads as something not yet done. The habit that catches it is mechanical rather than attentive: before building an arm, search the existing logs for the condition it would create — the position, the count, the target — and not for the name of the experiment that would create it. Arms are cheap to name and expensive to run, and the name is the one part that does not persist.

The same habit has a second form, which turned up within the hour of writing the first. Searching the archive by arm name finds every run of that arm and silently pools runs made under different conditions — §2.7 records what that cost. Both failures are one mistake: treating a name as an identifier for a condition. A name identifies an intention. The condition lives in the log beside it, and has to be read from there.

2.13 A score with a margin constraint has a ceiling, and comparing raw scores across it merges two effects

The clearest instance of §2.1’s error, and the one that changed a result. An unsupervised partition is scored by best-permutation agreement with the true labels — the diagonal of the label×cluster confusion matrix, maximised over assignments of clusters to labels. Reported alongside it is the share of items the busiest cluster claimed. The two are not independent. For any assignment the diagonal is bounded term by term by the smaller of its row and column mass, so with label frequencies wc and cluster shares qc the agreement cannot exceed Σc min(wc, qσ(c)), and for two balanced labels with busiest share q this is exactly:

agreement ≤ 1.5 − q

Checked on 216 creatures: no violations. It is not a vacuous bound either — a supervised control attains it exactly, and two of six arms sit within 0.004 of it. So the score splits into a ceiling set by how lopsided the partition is, and a slack against that ceiling which is the only part that is classification error at all.

The project had reported a difference between two conditions, reproducing on 18 of 18 creatures at 6.0 SE, as one effect across three pairs of arms. Decomposed, it is not the same effect in any two of them: in one pair the two ceilings are within 0.008 and the whole difference is classification; in another the ceilings differ by 0.055 and the classification difference is 0.001. One of the three pairs contains no classification difference whatsoever, and its number had been read as though it did. Two arms were also being compared as better and worse at the same task when, measured against their own ceilings, their errors were 0.004 and 0.187 — opposite ends of the scale the raw scores put next to each other.

The transferable rule is narrow and mechanical: when a statistic is a maximum subject to margins that vary between conditions, compare the slack, not the statistic. And the general shape is §2.1, §2.3 and §2.7 again — a reference point that exists but is not the one the claim is about. This is its fourth distinct instance here, which is why it is recorded as a class rather than as four separate mistakes.

2.14 An absence in the source is not an absence in the behaviour

Caught before it was published, and worth recording because the reasoning looked sound all the way to the conclusion. A mechanism was proposed that would only fire while the creature was asleep, so the first question was whether the relevant protocol sleeps at all — a fair question, since this project has already lost a result to a mechanism that could not fire in the window it was tested in. The protocol’s source contains no mention of sleep, of waking, or of the replay that accompanies it. On that basis the lead could have been closed for nothing.

It sleeps. Sleep here is driven by an internal fatigue variable crossing a threshold, so the host never calls for it and never needs to name it. The absence of the word meant “this code does not command it”, not “it does not happen”, and the two readings are indistinguishable from a search. What separates them is reading the mechanism that would produce the thing rather than searching for the name of it.

The general form is the one error this document keeps finding in new costumes: a reference point that exists but is not the one the claim is about. Here the claim was about a behaviour and the evidence was about a vocabulary. The habit that catches it is to ask what would have to be true in the kernel for the answer to be no, and then look there — which in this case took one grep and produced the opposite answer.

The lead did die, but on arithmetic rather than on absence, and the arithmetic is the interesting part. The sleep bout is fixed by two genome constants and works out at about three minutes against a roughly twenty-minute cycle, so the mechanism being proposed would run about fifteen percent of the time — and the mechanism it was to be grafted onto already runs continuously and costs nothing on either axis it was built to serve. Running a null fifteen percent as often is not a repair. That is a better refusal than the one the search would have given, and it also said what to build instead.

2.15 A guard that the mechanism is live is not a guard that the measurement can resolve it

The sharpest version of §2.2 yet, because the guards in question were written deliberately, in advance, and passed. An experiment asked whether a reward could be aimed at one phase of a self-generated two-phase movement. It carried two vacuity guards, both chosen before the run: one confirming the movement was happening, and one confirming the two phases were balanced rather than one having won outright. Both passed cleanly — the phase was reported on essentially every rewarded moment, and the split was 0.50.

Neither guard asked whether the reward could tell the two phases apart, which was the entire question. Credit is delivered through an eligibility trace, and the trace’s window is 2000 ms against a phase lasting about 136 ms — roughly fifteen phases per window, seven full cycles of the movement. Both halves sit inside every credit window, equally eligible, every time. So the two conditions being compared were the same condition with the labels permuted, and the null that came back measured the operating point rather than the thing the experiment was named after.

The distinction worth carrying: a vacuity guard normally asks whether the treatment happened. This failure needs a guard asking whether the instrument can see the treatment. Those are different questions and the first does not imply the second. The arithmetic that settles it was available before the run — three constants, all already measured — and cost nothing but the thought of comparing them.

Recorded with its chronology because that is the only part that makes it useful rather than embarrassing. The arithmetic was done and the prediction written down while the run was still executing: that the contrast would come back null, that a resolution guard would have voided it, and that a positive result would refute the whole argument. The guard was added before the log was opened, and run afterwards on the very configuration that produced the result it prints a refusal. A verdict that is wrong over correct numbers is recoverable; what makes it recoverable is having written the correction down before the numbers arrived.

2.16 A guard that the measurement can resolve the treatment is not a guard that the treatment did anything — and the positive control must be checked against the project’s own known negatives

§2.15 added a resolution guard to an experiment that had liveness guards and needed one more. The next run of that experiment carried all three — the movement is happening, the two phases are balanced, the credit window is short enough to tell them apart — plus a fourth added with them, that the credit can still arrive after the delay it has to survive. All four passed. The verdict printed a refusal. The refusal was not available.

What exposed it was not a guard but the results table. The measured quantity came back identical to a previous run of the same experiment, to within 0.5%, after the two quantities the run existed to vary had been changed twenty-fold and ten-fold respectively. A measured value that ignores both of its own causes is not measuring them. The null was equally consistent with the lesson having no effect at all, and nothing in four guards addressed that, because each one asked whether some part of the apparatus was working rather than whether the intervention had an outcome.

So the ladder has four rungs and they are all different questions. Is the mechanism present? Is it live? Can the instrument resolve it? And did the treatment produce any effect at all on anything? A positive control answers the fourth and only the fourth, and the first three do not imply it. Both halves of the quantity that answers it — error in the first third of the session and in the last third — had been recorded by the kernel all along and neither had ever been printed. Two runs reported where a lesson had landed without ever showing that one arrived.

And the control is easy to get wrong in two further ways, both of which were caught only while building it. First, it cannot be read on a treated arm: the error there is scored on a trial-mean output against a target that changes within the trial, so no single output can satisfy it and the control would have voided a working experiment. Second, and worse, the obvious candidate was a configuration this project had already measured as a null — a lesson aimed at a heard target, recorded years-equivalent earlier at −0.1% against +24% for a fixed one. A positive control that is already known not to work establishes nothing, and it would have voided every run of the experiment for a reason unconnected to the question. A positive control is a claim about the apparatus, so it has to be chosen against the record of what the apparatus is known to do, not against what it ought to do.

The control, once built correctly, passed at 6.3–18.4 SE on three genomes, so the refusal it was built to check survived. That is the outcome worth noticing: the guard did not overturn the result, it made the result defensible. The version without it was the same number with no entitlement to it.

2.17 A sharper statistic applied to the sample that produced a claim lands it on the bar

A lead stood at +0.0256 ± 0.0117 (2.2 SE) on 18 creatures: narrowing an eligibility window twentyfold appeared to improve a fixed-target lesson while delivering strictly less credit. Picking it up revealed that it had been measured loosely. The two genomes compared differ in exactly one scalar, which does not re-roll the wiring, so each seed names the same creature in both runs and the comparison was paired by construction — it had only ever been quoted unpaired because the per-creature quantity was never printed, existing solely as a mean. Pairing was therefore available for free, and the obvious move was to take it.

Had the existing data merely been re-analysed, which costs nothing and was the more obvious of the two moves, the result would have been published at the bar and would have been noise. The 3 SE threshold is a statement about sampling error in data the claim has not seen; it carries no protection when the claim and the sample share an origin, and a sharper instrument aimed at the discovery sample simply re-finds the discovery.

A second, narrower finding sits inside the same run and corrects an expectation stated before it: pairing bought almost nothing here, moving the pooled standard error only from 0.0086 to 0.0080, because the across-creature correlation between the two conditions is r = +0.133. A paired design converts between-unit variance into precision, so it pays in proportion to how strongly the units correlate, and here the measured quantity is dominated by within-unit noise rather than by which unit it is. What settled this question was doubling the creatures, not sharpening the statistic. The same structure of fact refused a two-pool readout elsewhere in this project, where only ρ = 0.101 of the relevant variance proved to be shared.

The lead itself is refused at 1.9 SE, and the distinction matters: this is a failure to replicate, not a demonstration of absence. The fresh half's interval extends to +0.032 and so cannot exclude the original effect — only fail to find it. The effect size fell 40% as the sample doubled, which is the same signature that retired an earlier reward manipulation in this project when it shrank between n = 12 and n = 16.

3. Credit assignment and interference

Teaching a second, conflicting vowel target destroys the first. The surviving fraction is 0.22. This section is what the project established about why, and about what does not fix it.

3.1 The wipe has a mechanism, and it is not a representational collision

Two lessons interfere while driving disjoint neuron groups, so the groups are not what they share. What they share is the reward: a single broadcast scalar nudges the bias of every neuron in the module, so teaching B random-walks A’s setting. Measured directly, B restores the very neurons A suppressed. Orthogonal targets are held simultaneously at 0.84 against the conflicting pair’s 0.22, which locates the failure in the conflict rather than in capacity.

3.2 It is an axis collision, not a distance effect

A second lesson that is silent about the first lesson’s axis is free; one that speaks to the same axis is destructive, confirmed at +11.5 SE. Distance between the two targets is not the variable.

3.3 A perfect per-neuron credit mask does not remove the wipe

An oracle mask — the host tells the creature which lesson is live, and the reward is confined to the half of the motor group that lesson uses — retains less than the broadcast rule it was meant to repair: +0.109 ± 0.057 against +0.320 ± 0.052, a deficit of −0.211 ± 0.074, negative at every gain floor. It also learns less, gaining +0.196 ± 0.015 against +0.362 ± 0.033. This retracts an earlier claim of the opposite sign, which had rested on the estimator described in §2.8.

3.6 Position and content are not separable in a binary half-split

A structural result, and a negative one about the design rather than the creature. The mask is a contiguous half of the motor group and each lesson prefers exactly one half, so within a phase “the mask is in the wrong place” and “the mask selects the wrong neurons” are the same statement. No contrast built from end-aligned masks can separate them.

The only mask aligned with neither lesson — the central half, which leaves each lesson half of its preferred neurons on every trial — does separate them, and cannot answer the retention question for a second structural reason: it cuts learning by a factor of 29 (gain +0.0125 ± 0.0023 against +0.362), so no creature clears even the lowest meaningful gain floor and retention has no denominator. Varying position far enough to matter destroys the learning whose retention one would be measuring. More creatures cannot fix this; it is not a power problem.

Extent is a second axis and potency gates it. An account in which extent is simply signed potency summed over the retained neurons predicts that restricting fewer wrong-end neurons does less damage; the half and the quarter at the wrong end are instead indistinguishable, at power to have seen a tenth of that. Where there is no potency, extent does nothing — not a reversal, nothing. §4.5 reaches the same square in the freezing family, and had predicted this cell before it was measured.

Learning rises monotonically with how much of the group the reward can reach, and the cheapest mask is no mask. The best-aimed quarter keeps 63% of the half’s learning on 57% of its neurons, so potency does not make a mask cheap — it makes a mask less dear than the same mask pointed the other way. Confinement is what costs; aim reduces that cost and never reverses it. So there is no mask in this family that credit assignment can afford, and the line closes on arithmetic rather than on anything about the creature.

The control is the entire design, and one part of the matching is exact while another is impossible. Effective credit is not, because eligibility sits in the active half and alignment is precisely what puts credit there, so the aligned arm delivers roughly twice as much usable credit. It is disclosed rather than corrected, which is the honest treatment of a matching that is impossible in principle rather than merely absent.

Same family as the one-sided test of §3.9, one run earlier.

3.11 Orthogonality and instant-specificity are mutually exclusive, and the phase-specific degree of freedom belongs to the generator rather than to the lesson

Every refused attempt to teach two moments of one self-generated movement had asked for two values of one output dimension. A separate, positive result sat beside it: two lessons on orthogonal dimensions coexist where same-dimension ones collide, 0.84 retained against 0.22, and the two dimensions are carried by two separate population-coded groups. So the two moments were given orthogonal lessons — one dimension asked of one phase, the other of the other — against a same-dimension baseline reproduced in the same run.

The design is flawed, and the flaw turns out to be structural. An orthogonal pair admits a phase-independent solution: satisfying both targets at all times satisfies each phase, so nothing ever required the creature to behave differently at two instants. Its null therefore cannot answer the question it was built for. And there is no patched version. If two lessons do not conflict, both can be satisfied at once and no instant-specific output is needed; if they do conflict, they conflict on one dimension, which is the baseline arm. Orthogonality and instant-specificity exclude each other by construction, so this route closes by argument rather than by measurement — a thing that cost one run and should have cost none.

What the run found instead is better than what it was built for. The orthogonal arms show a lesson effect of +0.0614 and +0.0670 (both past 8 SE) where the same-dimension arms show −0.0017 and +0.0001 — zero — with both pairs scored on single-dimension errors so the comparison is like for like, and the orthogonal arms effectively matching the ordinary fixed-target control at +0.0721. A lesson with a phase-independent solution is learned as well as any lesson. One without it is not learned at all: not partially, not slowly.

The reason lies in the preceding run. The only way to reduce error on a conflicting pair is to change the difference between the two phases’ outputs, and that difference was measured to be set by the competition strength — moving sixfold across its sweep — and invariant to teaching, with taught and free-running values agreeing to within a few per cent at every setting and the residuals changing sign. The phase-specific degree of freedom is owned by the oscillator and is not plastic. So the creature learns everything a phase lesson asks that can be satisfied without phase-specific output and nothing that requires it — not because reward cannot reach the right place or arrive at the right moment, both of which were handed over as oracles, but because the quantity the lesson would have to move is produced by a mechanism that reward does not write to. That is a mechanism for the whole refusal line, and it rests on two independent runs rather than on an argument.

A retraction nearly published, and the one question that stopped it. With a log-ratio error and two targets straddling the creature’s output, the summed error is exactly constant — verified to four decimals — and the two per-phase gradients are equal and opposite. That appeared to show the design carried 3% of an ordinary lesson’s drive, that the preceding runs had been judged against bars thirty times too tight, and that §3.10’s closure had to be withdrawn. It was the wrong quantity. The cancellation is on the mean; the experiment measures the difference, where the same two gradients add to twice a single lesson’s drive. The closure stands untouched. The check was one question — which quantity does this experiment actually measure? — asked before writing rather than after. A false retraction costs more than a false finding: it discards a valid result and sends the next several runs back over ground already covered.

4. Leverage and the geometry of the motor pool

4.1 The lesson silences the wrong side

Measured rather than fitted: a low formant target is reached by suppressing the upper half of the group, and a high target by suppressing the lower half — mirror-image profiles, both suppression. An earlier account in which the lesson raised the half it needed is a decisive reversal, and the reversal is what the rest of this section is an account of.

4.2 The readout is a centroid, so output potency is signed distance from it

The formant the host reads off a motor group is not a mean of rates. It is a rate-weighted centroid on a labelled line: neuron i carries a fixed position pi = (i − begin + ½) / n and the output is Σ(ripi) / Σri. Differentiating gives the influence of one neuron on behaviour with no free parameter:

∂F / ∂ri = (pi − c) / Σrj     c = the output currently produced

So potency is distance from the centroid the group is currently at — zero for a neuron sitting on it, largest at the ends, and state-dependent, moving as c moves. That immediately explains a documented out-of-sample failure: a leverage law fitted to three single-neuron costs with one free parameter reproduced them to 1.68 percentage points and then failed two half-block costs. The fit was a function of position alone, which is adequate while c barely moves — as it does not between single-neuron blocks — and wrong once a whole half of the group is blocked and c moves a long way. Influence is not a property of a neuron; it is a property of a neuron and the current state.

The magnitude |pi − c| is nevertheless refuted, by a run that was already on disk when the derivative was written down. One neuron blocked at three positions, 36 creatures: the top (p = 0.964) costs 30% of the lesson at −5.8 SE, the middle (0.464) costs 7% at −1.1 SE, the bottom (0.036) costs 9% at −1.4 SE. Middle and bottom are both null and indistinguishable from each other, and no single centroid makes a symmetric magnitude produce that: at c = 0.093 it requires the middle to cost about six times the bottom, and at c ≈ 0.5 it requires the bottom to cost like the top.

Potency is (pi − c) taken with the sign of the required direction of travel. A lesson that must lower the centroid can only do so by suppressing high-position neurons, so the far end on that side carries the lever; the centroid itself has no lever in either direction; and the opposite end is on the wrong side, because suppressing it would move the output the wrong way, so the lesson was never going to use it.

The qualitative half of this was written before the run that measured it, in the pre-registration of that experiment: index is position, a weighted mean is least sensitive near its own centre, and the end the lesson travels toward should matter more. What is new is the derivative itself, the state-dependence, and the sign — and the first arithmetic written from it, the symmetric one, was wrong.

4.7 The larynx reads nine scalars

The motor interface is nine scalar groups, and the object identity being taught sits at the null in all eighteen vocal measurements taken. Reordering the vocal population by its own response difference lifts discriminability, so the information is present in the slice and absent from the readout.

5. The voice and the body

5.1 The voice drones

There is no syllabic rhythm in the produced voice. A +9.99 dB modulation peak that indicated otherwise was a fit on the knee of a low-pass filter, not a spectral feature of the signal. Relatedly, a 3 Hz ring in the voice is the product of two filters — a fast rate constant and a gate smoothing term — and may be no oscillator at all; it scales threefold with self-hearing, and a deaf creature still rings at +3.1 SE, so self-audition is a gain on it rather than its cause.

5.2 The frame is a body part

A single finding that explains five separate refusals at once: the apparent syllabic frame is a resonant jaw at about 4.5 Hz, a property of the articulator rather than a central clock. Three independent routes from a frame to content are closed by it, and a jaw-gate mechanism that appeared to pass both halves of its test turned out to have a mis-set anchor, with the creature’s own silences outperforming the imposed gate.

5.3 A blind controller beats feedback

A controller with no access to the sensed formant outperforms one with it: reach is immune to loop delay and a 905 ms loop is not. The precision advantage claimed for feedback was cleared by a sensorless controller, and an uninformative feature leaves only the intercept, which is the blind controller in disguise. Altered auditory feedback moves production by +0.01.

5.4 Intrinsic plasticity is the backlash

Formant actuator hysteresis measures 107 Hz with intrinsic plasticity enabled and 2.0 Hz with it off: the plant moves under its own controller. The backlash is per-neuron — pooling the regulator takes hysteresis from 107 Hz to 7 Hz and gives back 30 Hz of reach.

5.5 The ear hears its own formant and does not use it

The self-produced first formant is present in the auditory representation (+0.124 and +0.114 on two seed families) and unused by the motor system. The ear’s estimate is well described by a fixed attenuation plus noise, with the attenuation free and the noise 56 Hz input-referred; the signal is localised, one frequency bin of 32 carrying 92% of the available benefit, which is destroyed by pooling. So the failure of closed-loop control here is a missing mapping, not a missing signal.

6. Representation and capacity

6.1 Vocabulary does not collapse, and nine words do not cost naming

Advantage over chance rises with vocabulary size rather than falling. At nine words against two six-word arrangements, total naming excess is conserved (−0.012 ± 0.019 and +0.020 ± 0.018, opposite-sign nulls at n = 36). There is one real cost and it is not naming: delivered formant separation falls by 11.8 ± 3.5 Hz at matched trials per word, 3.3 SE. The shape of the word arrangement does not matter (−0.032 ± 0.027), so there is no preferred axis per creature.

6.2 The ceiling is an exponent

Delivered formant change scales with the aligned component of the learned bias as a power law with exponent about 0.61. Moving delivery by a factor requires moving the bias by a larger factor, which closes the naming line quantitatively rather than by argument: a commitment brake moved the aligned bias 1.07× where 6.2× was needed.

6.3 The derived index gap: two different results, and the boundary was the cause of the one that mattered

The creature derives its own context from an auditory rate average, and the resulting index is reliably worse when the teaching target tracks the heard word than when it is fixed, on 18 of 18 creatures — a gap of −0.056 ± 0.009, 6.0 SE. Three candidate causes were tested and all three are dead: gate corruption has the wrong sign; silencing self-audition does not close the gap but widens it by 0.037 ± 0.015, under a test with power to see a closure; and a drift account is excluded both by its own point prediction, 3.9 SE away, and by the learning arm decaying least.

Decomposed against the occupancy ceiling of §2.13, the 18-of-18 result is two unrelated effects. For the auditory-rate source the two conditions have ceilings within 0.008 of each other, so essentially the whole −0.056 is classification error. For the running-average source the ceilings differ by 0.055 while the classification difference is 0.001 — both of its arms are pinned to their ceilings, so its identically-sized gap is entirely a matter of one partition being more lopsided than the other, with no confusion difference in it at all. The gap is real in both; it is the same number for different reasons, and no single account can be correct for both.

What remains unexplained is therefore narrower than the original statement: one classification gap, in one source, between two arms that share the clustering rule, the feature that rule reads, and the distribution of that feature. Any candidate that is a property of the rule is excluded structurally — it would shift both arms equally and cannot produce a difference between them — which retires two of the candidates on the list without measuring either.

One candidate survives that filter, and measured by intervention it accounts for the whole of the remaining gap. The window in which the partition accumulates its feature, and the moment it commits an update, are decided by whether the creature’s own larynx is below its setpoint — a deliberate choice, since vocal silence marks listening where auditory activity does not. Because that boundary is an internal read of motor rate, silencing self-audition never touched it: the intervention that refused the self-contamination account cut the acoustic path only.

Handing the boundary to the host — the caregiver’s own flag for when it is speaking, with the feature, the clustering rule and the latch all unchanged — takes classification error to exactly zero. All four arms built that way sit precisely on their own occupancy ceilings, under both an alternating and a shuffled word order, so the margin-constrained score of §2.13 is attained rather than merely approached. The classification gap closes completely, −0.061 ± 0.011 at 5.5 SE, under a test whose power to see a closure was checked first. So the feature is exonerated and so is the clustering rule; the boundary was the whole of it.

The mechanism had been sitting in the kernel’s own commentary for a month, unconnected to the gap: a fragment holding only silence writes a prototype from silence and overwrites the latch. The larynx gate fires several ragged episodes per trial and some are such fragments. That also explains why the effect was a difference rather than a level: in the condition that produces a word-dependent voice, the fragmentation is word-correlated, while in the control it is word-independent noise.

What remains, once confusion is gone, is a decay rather than a standing imbalance, and the clean boundary is what made it visible. Under the oracle window the partition’s lopsidedness is 0.606 to 0.609 across thirty-six creatures — zero variance, so not an outcome individual creatures succeed or fail at — and the index runs 0.919 in the first third of a session to 0.858 in the last, −0.060 ± 0.001 at 46.5 SE, on every arm built that way. The arms using the creature’s own boundary do not decay at all. Since classification error is zero throughout, the partition is becoming progressively more lopsided as the session proceeds, which is what a prototype whose learning rate has reached zero looks like under an input that is still moving.

That account had been tested and excluded, against its own point prediction, at 3.9 SE. It was tested on the arms using the creature’s own boundary — which sit far below their ceiling with a fifth of the score lost to classification error, and so have no headroom in which a decay could appear. The account was not wrong; it was measured in the one condition that could not exhibit it. This is the reference-point error of §2.1 and §2.3 one level up: not the wrong baseline, the wrong regime. The transferable form is that a null is only as strong as the headroom the measurement had, and headroom is a quantity that can be computed in advance — §2.13’s ceiling is exactly such a computation.

And the boundary is worth the whole of the behaviour, not just the carrier. The quantity the context machinery exists for is how far the creature moves its first formant per word. With its own derived index and a clean boundary it delivers 76.8 ± 5.0 Hz against a control arm’s 23.4, a conditional effect of +53.4 ± 5.6 Hz at 9.6 SE where the creature’s own boundary gives +12.0 ± 4.5. Measured against a creature that is simply told which word it heard, the difference is −1.0 ± 6.0 Hz — statistically identical, where the same comparison without the clean boundary is −46.9 ± 5.0, 9.4 SE below. So knowing which word it is buys nothing beyond knowing when a word is happening. The matched-marginal control sits below the no-index arm, so this is word-specific delivery rather than a general increase in vocal motion.

That is a trade of oracles rather than the removal of one, and it is worth being exact about: an expensive oracle, the word’s identity, has been replaced by a cheap one, its timing, at no measured cost. The cheap one is also the one the system has a real detector for — the creature goes nearly silent while it listens, voiced fraction 0.276 to 0.010, which is shipped and measured. What fails is not the signal but the gate on it: an instantaneous threshold on a noisy mean, with no hysteresis, which chatters and splits one word into several episodes of which some contain only silence.

Two things this does not say. The boundary was handed over by an oracle, so what is measured is the size of the prize for building a boundary detector rather than a detector — and §6.5 reports what happened when four detectors were built to collect it. And the decay above is named but not fixed: nothing here shows that restoring the prototype’s learning rate recovers the 0.060 — indeed the behaviour reaches the oracle’s level while still decaying, which bounds how much that 0.060 can be costing.

Two side results survived that line. Deafening improves the index in the control arm only (+0.030 ± 0.004, 7.1 SE), and makes the tracking arm’s partition more lopsided (+0.066 ± 0.018, 3.7 SE). Separately, the derived context carrier is refuted directly: the index never separates the two lessons (0.23 against 0.23), while the auditory feature it is built from carries the word at 97.8% on held-out trials where the reward lands. The feature is fine; the extraction is not.

6.4 Two percent of a percent of the ticks does the work

0.2% of ticks account for the learning, and no schedule reproduces it at 3.3 SE over 32 creatures. The result carries no lesson about which ticks, which is stated here because it is the kind of finding that invites one.

The last column says why, and it is the more durable half of the result. For two balanced words the index match cannot exceed 1.5 − occupancy, where occupancy is the share of trials falling in the busier half of the table; slack is the accuracy an arm still has available. The detector-free arm's slack is 0.006 and the handed-boundary arm's is 0.000. The leading arms are already as accurate as their own partitions permit, and every arm in the table lands where its ceiling puts it. The arms that lose are exactly the arms sitting below their ceilings. So the open problem is not when does a word happen but where to put the threshold so the trials divide evenly — a different question, and one no gate in this family was built to answer.

Three things this does not say. Splitting the trials evenly is shown to be necessary for a derived index to beat the detector-free arm, not sufficient: for an arm at its ceiling index ≡ 1.5 − occupancy identically, so accuracy and balance are the same number and this run cannot separate them. The boundary prize itself is genome-dependent — handed-boundary minus detector-free is +11.1 ± 7.1 Hz here and +27.4 ± 7.0 on the earlier family, and the two runs share no creatures, so §6.3's figure and this one are two samples of a range rather than a disagreement. And the two gate shapes built for this run were very nearly refused on a short smoke that mis-measured them: their gate-events-per-trial read 0.55 and 0.59 against a design of 1.0 on a 17×-short run and 1.76 and 1.86 at full budget, because a rate-thresholded gate's behaviour depends on a rate distribution that is still settling in the first few percent of a creature's life. Only a handed window was budget-invariant.

7. Transmission between creatures

7.1 One creature teaches another: a learned distinction crosses to a second individual through the air, and narrows on the way

Everything else in this document is one creature and a host. This is two creatures. Generation 0 is taught by the host in the shipped way; what it actually produces for each of the two words is measured; and generation 1 — a different individual, its own wiring and its own noise from its own seed — both hears those vowels and is rewarded toward them. No genome is passed and nothing is bred.

The design is shaped around a refusal rather than a hope. Teaching toward a heard target is a measured null here: reward cuts error by 24% toward a fixed target and by 0.1% toward a heard one. So “imitate what you hear” would have failed for a reason already known and said nothing about transmission. The tutor therefore supplies both channels, which is what being taught by someone means.

Transmission: +53.3 ± 10.0 Hz, 5.3 SE. The two arms it is drawn from differ in nothing but which tutor vowel goes with which word — identical acoustics, identical spread — so the quantity is the information in the tutor’s voice and not its sound. Both generations’ lessons demonstrably landed, at 28.2 and 4.5 SE, so the fourth rung is satisfied before the verdict is read.

And the result is symmetric, which is stronger than the contrast alone. The swapped arm did not come back at zero. It came back at −27.7, a near mirror image of the tutored arm’s +25.6. The pupil learns an inverted mapping about as readily as the true one. That rules out the reading where one arm is simply broken, and it is what transmission actually means: the pupil acquires whatever convention its tutor happens to hold, with no preference for the one the host originally taught.

It narrows, and most of the narrowing is the tutor being quiet rather than the channel being lossy. The host demanded a 460 Hz distinction; generation 0 produced 96.1; the tutored pupil produced 25.6 — a total shortfall of 70.5 ± 9.2 Hz across one generation. But a tutor hands on far less than it was asked for, so the pupil is being set a much narrower task than its tutor was. Restoring the host’s spread around the tutor’s own vowel centre recovers to 61.0, +35.4 ± 13.6 Hz over the tutored arm (2.6 SE): directional, below the 3 SE bar, and not claimed as established. The residual against a host-taught creature, 35.1 ± 12.4 Hz, is 2.8 SE and likewise not claimed. So whether anything is lost beyond the width of what the tutor says is genuinely unresolved here, and the honest reading is that the narrowing is dominated by a quantity the experiment also measures.

Three limits, stated rather than left to be found. This is one generation. Whether the ratio is stable, decays to nothing, or stabilises at a narrow convention is what iterated learning is about, and the run cannot say; a second generation is the same code with the tape taken from the pupil. The ratios quoted above are ratios of means and are descriptive only — the per-creature paired ratio with a jackknife is the statistic, and it was not computed, so no proportion here carries an interval. And the tape carries formants only: pitch stays the host’s, because the creature’s own f0 is not recorded. That is a limit of the recording rather than a claim that pitch does not matter.

One design error, caught by a smoke run and worth keeping. The first version handed on 621 and 620 Hz — a 0.3 Hz distinction — because the default taught arm is the heard-target mode this project had already measured at 0.1%. A tutor with nothing to say cannot teach, and the run would have been void by construction while looking like a clean negative. The fix was to give both generations the word-conditional configuration every distinction here is measured under: the tutor so it has something to hand on, and the pupil so it can structurally hold it. Giving it only to the tutor would have tested whether a creature can learn something it has no way to represent.

7.2 Down a chain the convention decays to nothing — but a width-limited channel with no information loss would look the same, and that is not yet separated

§7.1 established transmission between two individuals and left one generation of narrowing. One step cannot tell a decay that reaches zero from a convention settling at some narrow width, and those mean opposite things about whether anything can be held between creatures. Four steps separate them. Each generation is a different individual, taught from the previous generation’s own production.

It decays. Generations 3 and 4 are both within 3 SE of zero. The last generation’s own lesson landed at 4.3 SE, so the final zero is a creature that learned something and had nothing to learn — not one that failed to learn. Culture starts and does not accumulate. The first two generations reproduce the preceding experiment to the digit on the same seeds, which is what licenses reading the deeper ones at all.

What is deliberately not claimed. Two step ratios are resolved, 0.266 ± 0.084 and 0.409 ± 0.157, and they are not distinguishable from each other (0.8 SE) — so there is no claim that the loss per step is constant and none that it changes. Two points do not carry a curve. The deeper ratios are suppressed rather than printed, because a quotient by something indistinguishable from zero invites a reader to take a sign from noise; an earlier build of the same experiment printed one such ratio as “+4.367 ± 48.071”.

A pre-registered test that came in below its bar, and why. The control was a positive prediction rather than a null: flipping the two labels at every step should make the sign alternate down the chain, which is far harder to produce by accident. The registered statistic — the mean of (predicted sign × magnitude) over all four generations — came in at 2.7 SE, short of 3. The per-generation signs are correct for generations 1, 2 and 3 and wrong for 4, and generation 4 is precisely where the faithful chain is already at zero: a generation with nothing left to invert. So the summary was diluted by its own later terms, which is a property of the statistic chosen and not a finding. Inversion itself is not in doubt — the flipped arm at generation 1 sits 4.3 SE from zero and reproduces the previous run. The lesson is about summary statistics: one number averaged over a series that dies halfway through is the wrong summary, and choosing it in advance does not make it the right one.

And the decay does not establish a lossy channel. §7.1 left open whether anything is lost beyond the width of what a tutor says; the two contrasts that would have settled it came in at 2.6 and 2.8 SE, both below bar. A tutor hands on a narrower distinction than it was asked for, so each pupil is set a harder-to-hit task than its own tutor was — and a width-limited channel losing no information at all produces a decaying series too. “It decays” and “the channel leaks” are different claims and only the first is measured here. Separating them is the same code with each generation’s tape widened to a fixed spread around its own tutor’s centre: if the series then holds, the loss was width.

Two guards were added between the smoke run and the real one, and the first is the reason this result can be stated at all. An earlier build printed a confident “it decays” over a chain whose generation zero had no distinction in it — noise propagating through four generations, where “it decays” would have been the most confidently wrong sentence available. The guard now voids the run unless the first generation has something to transmit, checked before the series is interpreted rather than after. It is the same failure the preceding experiment had in its own first version, which is the argument for writing the guard into the experiment instead of remembering to look.

On the comparison with human iterated learning: artificial languages passed down chains of human learners become more structured, the opposite direction to this. The comparison is offered as loose and should be read that way — that quantity is structure and learnability, this one is the magnitude of a single acoustic distinction, and a magnitude that shrinks is not the same object as a grammar that regularises. What the two share is the method, not the measurement.

7.3 The decay was a width limit, not a lossy channel — and holding the width fixed turns transmission into a ratchet that locks in arbitrary conventions

§7.2 found the convention decaying to nothing down a chain and could not say whether the channel loses information or whether each tutor simply says less than it was asked, handing its pupil a smaller task. The separation: carry the tutor’s own vowel centre and its own ordering, and re-spread the pair to the original log distance. The mapping propagates; the width is held fixed. A third chain exchanges the inherited ordering at every step, because a re-imposed width hands every pupil a wide target whatever its tutor said, and without that control a surviving series could carry nothing inherited at all.

It was width. With the width held, generation 4 carries 85.6 ± 5.5 against generation 0’s 96.1 ± 5.1 — a difference of 1.4 SE, no detectable loss across four generations — where the plain chain lost 16.0 SE worth over the same span. Culture here does not decay because the channel loses information; it decays because each tutor produces a narrower distinction than it was taught, and the next pupil can only aim at what it hears. That relocates the limit from transmission to production.

And the mechanism turns out to be a ratchet, which nothing predicted. Five of the eighteen chains ended holding the inverted convention at full strength, and eighteen of eighteen never changed sign after the first pupil. The inverted chains are precisely those whose first pupil nearly failed: magnitude 5.1 ± 1.8 at generation 1 against 86.4 ± 7.5 for the rest, a difference of 10.5 SE. When a pupil lands near zero, which way round its pair came out is noise — and the widened tape takes that sign, restores it to full width, and hands it on, after which it never reverses. The magnitude series shows the amplification directly, rising from 63.8 at generation 1 to 88.3 at generation 2 as the near-failures are widened. The mechanism does not merely preserve a convention; it restores the magnitude of whatever convention it finds, including one that began as noise. An arbitrary convention arising from a near-tie and then held stably down a lineage is a recognisable phenomenon rather than a malfunction — and the sharpest available reason to distrust a single chain, since a quarter of them ended up with a different language.

The verdict this run printed was wrong, and the error has the same shape as the one in §7.2. It read the signed mean, 35.5 ± 19.7, which fails a 3 SE bar — because a chain confidently holding the inverted convention contributes a large negative number that cancels against the faithful ones. A signed mean across units is not a measure of magnitude when the sign can differ between units, and the mean absolute value separates “the distinction shrank” from “some units hold the opposite convention” — two different findings that the registered statistic merged into one wrong sentence. That is now twice in consecutive runs that a pre-registered statistic was the wrong shape for its data. Pre-registration prevents fitting a test to the results. It does nothing about choosing a test incapable of expressing them. No re-run was required: the data were complete and the correction is arithmetic on them, so the corrected condition was evaluated against the run’s own numbers and the source fixed for next time, rather than spending two hours re-deriving numbers that were already right under a sentence that was not.

9. Provenance

Every number above comes from a run of the project’s own experiment binary, gated before execution on --experiment verify reporting pass with the pinned hash ad96f882becbee92 and a passing pinned-hash check. Runs that failed the gate are discarded rather than reported.

Sample sizes are creatures, not trials: a creature is one genome with one seed, grown and taught from birth. Where a paired statistic is quoted, creatures are matched by seed across arms, which is licensed by the per-job seed being a function of the repetition index alone and independent of the arm — verified by a shared baseline arm reproducing bit-identically across three separate multi-hour runs, and by a direct cross-run check of that arm against itself returning +0.0000 ± 0.0000 over 32 creatures.

Retention figures are a ratio of sums with a leave-one-out jackknife standard error, swept over a floor on per-creature learning, and are reported only where the effect holds at every floor retaining at least three creatures in both arms. Section 2.8 explains why the more obvious estimator is not used.

Appendix A. Excluded by the criterion

These project results are omitted from the body because a published result says or frames them first. They are listed so that the inclusion criterion of §1 can be checked.

One entry needs its line drawn explicitly. The idea that behaviour depends on some directions in a population code and not others is Kaufman and colleagues’, and it is excluded. What §4.2 claims is narrower and is included: in this system the map from population activity to behaviour is analytically known rather than fitted, so the potent direction can be differentiated instead of estimated, it is state-dependent, and its sign — not its magnitude — is what the measurements require.

The same line, drawn once more for §2.13. The scoring rule is Kuhn and Munkres’s assignment problem and is excluded; that a uniform cluster prior hurts on imbalanced data is Assran and colleagues’ and is excluded. What is included is the consequence of fixing that score’s margins — the ceiling, its exactness, and the fact that it splits a result this project had published as one effect into two with different mechanisms. The bound is elementary once stated; nobody here had stated it, and the cost of not stating it was a candidate priced against the wrong arm by a factor of twenty-five.
